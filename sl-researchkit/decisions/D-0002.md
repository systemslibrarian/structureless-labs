Decision ID: D-0002 (sl-researchkit)
Question:     Should the lab document the lineage from crypto-lab-scloud-vault — a prior
              project explaining Wang et al.'s S-Cloud+ — in this repo?
Alternatives: (A) Document it as competence on-ramp ONLY, with explicit non-endorsement
                  fencing so no reader can confuse "explained someone else's published KEM"
                  with "validated our own experimental KEM."
              (B) Document it broadly (mention the link without honesty fencing).
              (C) Don't document it; let visitors discover the prior project independently
                  via the owner's GitHub.
Chosen:       (A) — competence on-ramp with explicit non-endorsement fencing.
Reason:       The provenance of the lab's "structureless" thesis is verifiable, real
              engagement with the published structureless-lattice literature. Making that
              engagement visible is more honest than letting visitors infer it from
              outside the repo. Option B carries a credibility-transfer risk ("their KEM
              is just a study of a published one") that the lab's bet — method over
              primitive — does not need and should not absorb. Option C leaves a true
              and load-bearing fact off the record, which violates principle 6
              (Provenance: every significant decision has a flight-recorder entry).
Risks:        A reader could still misread the lineage as endorsement. Mitigated by an
              explicit non-endorsement sentence in the new README "Lineage and related
              prior work" section, and by a parallel fenced "Related (for study, not
              endorsement)" line in sl-kem/README.md.
Evidence:     Grade D (framing / provenance decision, not a measured result).
Reviewed by:  Principal Investigator — surfaced the question (the lab's credibility
                argument is *stronger* when the engagement is visible than when it is
                implicit). VERDICT: PASS.
