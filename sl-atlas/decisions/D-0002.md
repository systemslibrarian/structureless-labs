Decision ID: D-0002 (sl-atlas)
Question:     Should the `encoding` atlas concept gain a live interactive,
              and if so, *which* encoding family should the live demo
              actually simulate? Three families are described in the
              concept's three views: (i) bitwise modular, (ii) Reed-Muller,
              (iii) Barnes-Wall `BW_{2^k}`. A demo that *claimed* to
              simulate all three would either be much larger than the
              first interactive ([D-0001 sl-atlas](D-0001.md)) or would
              quietly fake the harder families.
Alternatives: (A) No interactive. The three-depth prose already explains the
                  trade-off; adding a demo invites the "simulate all three"
                  trap.
              (B) Simulate **all three** families honestly: implement a real
                  Reed-Muller `RM(r, m)` encoder/decoder and a Barnes-Wall
                  `BW_{2^k}` encoder/decoder, plus the bitwise baseline.
              (C) Simulate **bitwise modular only**, with the prose and a
                  visible non-claim block making explicit that RM and BW
                  are not simulated and why.
Chosen:       **(C)** — bitwise modular only, with a visible
              "What this does NOT simulate" note in the interactive itself,
              cross-linking to this decision.
Reason:       Bitwise modular is the simplest member of the family the
              developer view discusses, and it is the one whose lesson
              (decoding radius `τ = q/4`; failure when `|e| > τ`) maps
              one-to-one onto a 1-D ring visualization. Reed-Muller's
              decoding radius improvement comes from joint decoding over
              `n > 1` coordinates and Barnes-Wall's improvement from a
              dense lattice in `2^k` dimensions; neither fits a 1-D ring
              honestly, and a 2-D or higher visualization would multiply
              the implementation cost and the chance of teaching a wrong
              intuition. Option B (simulate everything) was rejected on
              the same ground the LWE interactive declines to claim
              "secure at toy sizes": doing the wrong demo well teaches
              the wrong lesson. Option A (no interactive) was rejected
              because the bitwise-only case carries genuine pedagogical
              load — the exact CBD probability mass function across the
              decoding boundary is a *quantitative* picture the prose
              cannot draw.
Risks:        (i) **A reader may think the demo represents the whole
                family.** Mitigated by the visible non-claim block (the
                `enc-non-claim` panel in the rendered interactive) and
                the cross-link to this decision from the interactive's
                own text.
              (ii) **The single-coordinate δ undersells the real KEM's δ.**
                Real δ accumulates noise across `n` coordinates and across
                the KEM round (key + ciphertext noise). Mitigated by an
                explicit sentence in the δ-panel paragraph that names this
                gap and points back to the [Noise](https://systemslibrarian.github.io/structureless-labs/#/noise)
                and [Parameter choices](https://systemslibrarian.github.io/structureless-labs/#/parameter-choices)
                concepts.
              (iii) **The CBD PMF computation is a place a bug could lie
                undetected.** Mitigated by a verification step at decision
                time: η=1 and η=2 PMFs were computed and matched against
                the closed-form values ({1/4, 1/2, 1/4} for η=1;
                {1/16, 4/16, 6/16, 4/16, 1/16} for η=2), and Σ_k Pr[X=k] = 1
                was checked for η ∈ [1, 6]. If a future change touches
                `cbdPmf()`, that verification must be re-run; consider
                extending `scripts/check-lwe-math.mjs` to also assert
                CBD-PMF invariants.
Evidence:     Grade D — design / explainability decision, not a measured
              security result. Verification evidence: PMF closed-form
              cross-check and total-probability check at decision time.
Reviewed by:  Teacher — VERDICT: PASS. The bitwise-only decision lets the
                concept reach the *quantitative* picture (live δ, PMF
                bars colored as failure mass) that the prose alone could
                not draw. The non-claim block is the load-bearing piece
                that prevents miscalibration; without it the verdict
                would be BLOCK.
              Cryptographer — VERDICT: PASS. Bit-by-bit decoding radius
                `τ = q/4` is the textbook value (cited under [`encoding`
                researcher view](https://systemslibrarian.github.io/structureless-labs/#/encoding)).
                CBD is the constant-time-friendly noise distribution
                preferred by Kyber, FrodoKEM, and SPEC-000's default
                error distribution choice — using it here keeps the
                visualization aligned with what real KEMs actually use.
              Attacker — VERDICT: PASS. The δ-panel correctly visualizes
                the *failure mass* (the bars outside ±τ), which is the
                exact knob D'Anvers-Vercauteren-Verbauwhede (2019)'s
                adaptive-failure attack exploits. A reader can see why
                a non-zero δ is attackable, not just inconvenient.
              Engineer — VERDICT: PASS. Pure vanilla JS, no new
                dependencies, no build step introduced; WAI-ARIA
                radiogroup keyboard navigation is implemented per the
                same pattern as the LWE interactive's preset row.
                Reused mulberry32 + sampleCBD locally inside the new
                mount function (deliberate duplication kept under 20
                lines per helper; not yet worth hoisting to a shared
                module — the file is still a single-IIFE shape).
              Archivist — VERDICT: PASS. The concept's prose
                (`content/encoding.json` and the matching entry in

              Principal Investigator — VERDICT: PASS. The research

                quantitative piece the LWE demo did not carry, so the
                two interactives teach distinct lessons — not the
                same lesson twice.
