Question:     Three coupled scope questions from the 2026-07-11 learner-
              journey round: (1) May the attack-cost explorer ship a
              security-estimate model in the browser, given the lab's
              "numbers without methodology are not results" rule? (2) May
              the lattice visualizer present Babai round-off as the CVP
              lesson, knowing round-off is a heuristic that occasionally
              misses even with a reduced basis? (3) Should
              `sl-atlas/src/concepts.js` become a generated artifact?
Alternatives: (1a) No cost model — prose only, link to the external
                   lattice-estimator project.
              (1b) Ship the simplified primal/GSA/core-SVP estimate, with
                   the simplification named in the UI, reference points
                   labeled as the model's own output (never the schemes'
                   official claims), and a CI regression test pinning the
                   model's behavior.
              (2a) Use Babai nearest-plane and present success as
                   guaranteed.
              (2b) Use round-off, and make the occasional good-basis miss
                   part of the lesson (a "near-boundary miss" outcome
                   distinct from the skewed-basis failure).
              (3a) Keep hand-mirroring concepts.js from content/.
              (3b) Generate concepts.js from content/ with a --check CI
                   gate.
Chosen:       **(1b), (2b), (3b)**.
Reason:       (1b) The rule is "numbers WITHOUT methodology are not
              results." The explorer ships the methodology alongside every
              number: the success condition is printed in the UI, the
              simplifications are enumerated in the What-this-is-not note,
              and the reference dots are explicitly the model's output. At
              introduction the model lands within a few bits of the widely
              cited core-SVP figures (β=406 → 2^118.6 for the Kyber-512
              shape against the published 2^118), which is what makes it
              honest enough to teach with; scripts/check-cost-model.mjs
              pins that behavior. (2a) was rejected because it would trade
              a true statement for a clean one: round-off's basis-quality
              sensitivity IS the concept being taught, and the measured
              asymmetry (94/100 exact reduced vs 21/100 exact skewed on the
              fixed CI sweep) teaches it honestly, near-boundary misses
              included. (3b) closes a real wound: the hand-mirrored files
              had already drifted (emphasis markers and a link lost in five
              concepts, found and repaired during this round) — "content
              authored once" was a principle the tooling did not enforce
              until now.
Risks:        The cost model could be mistaken for an authoritative
              estimator despite the labeling. Mitigated by the reading-band
              labels ("category-1 territory"), the ±-nothing absence of
              official-claim comparisons in the UI, and the concept's own
              self-check question that asks WHY the model's numbers differ
              from official levels. The generated concepts.js could tempt
              hand-edits; the GENERATED header and the --check CI gate make
              that a failing build rather than a silent fork.
Evidence:     Grade B for the model-calibration claim (measured against
